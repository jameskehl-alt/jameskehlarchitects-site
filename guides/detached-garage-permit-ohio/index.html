Do I need a permit for a detached garage in Ohio?

Usually, yes. A detached garage is an accessory building under the Residential Code of Ohio, and where a certified residential building department covers your property it needs plan approval and a building permit before work starts. Only one-story sheds and similar buildings of 200 square feet or less are exempt. A garage almost always needs zoning approval too, from the city, village or township.

By James Kehl, RA, Ohio registered architect, James Kehl Architects. Published 15 November 2026. Rules vary by place: check with your local zoning and building offices.

Which code covers a detached garage?

The Residential Code of Ohio (RCO). Its scope, section 101.2, covers one-, two- and three-family houses and "any accessory structure incidental to that dwelling house", which is what a detached garage is. Two exceptions matter:

  • A garage used for a business, such as a contractor's shop or a repair garage, falls under the Ohio Building Code instead, with stricter rules and sealed drawings. The guide What permits does a commercial build-out need in Ohio? covers that side.
  • A building used for farming on farmland is outside Ohio's building codes altogether. The guide Ohio's agricultural building exemption, explained sets out the tests.

Is any garage small enough to skip the permit?

In practice, no. RCO section 102.10 exempts "one-story detached accessory structures used as tool and storage sheds, playhouses and similar uses" with a floor area of 200 square feet or less. A garage that holds a car is nearly always larger than that, so plan on a permit. Some cities set a smaller limit in their own code, so a shed close to 200 square feet is worth a call as well.

What if no residential building department covers my property?

Then there is no residential building permit to get. RCO section 101.5 says that where no residential building department certified by the Ohio Board of Building Standards has jurisdiction, the owner does not have to submit construction documents, seek approvals, request inspections or obtain a certificate of occupancy. This is common in rural townships. The garage still has to meet zoning where the township has zoning, and a lender or insurer may still want drawings. The Board of Building Standards' building department map shows who, if anyone, covers your address.

What does zoning look at?

Where the garage sits and how big it is. Each city, village and township sets its own numbers, but zoning for an accessory building usually limits:

  • the setback from each property line, and sometimes the distance from the house;
  • the height, often lower than the house;
  • the floor area, or the share of the lot that buildings may cover;
  • whether it may stand in the front yard;
  • in some towns, how it looks, such as materials that match the house.

The zoning office will ask for a site plan drawn to scale. The guide What does a zoning site plan need in Ohio? lists what to show.

What drawings does a detached garage permit need?

Most departments ask for a site plan with the setbacks, a floor plan with the door and window sizes, elevations showing the height, and a wall section or framing details: the footings below the frost depth your department sets, the slab, the wall framing, the headers over the garage doors, the roof trusses or rafters and how the building is anchored. If the garage will be wired, the electrical work is shown too. Some cities publish their own list; the City of Columbus and the City of Lakewood, for example, each have a detached garage handout. Read your department's list before anything is drawn.

What about wiring, plumbing or a room above?

Wiring and plumbing usually need their own permits and inspections. A room, a bathroom or an apartment over the garage is a bigger question: zoning may not allow a second dwelling on the lot, and living space changes what the code asks of the building. Ask the zoning office first, before the design is drawn around it.

Do I need an architect to draw a detached garage?

Not by state law; Ohio does not require an architect's seal on drawings for residential buildings and their garages. What you need is a set the zoning and building reviewers can approve the first time. Short answers

Do I need a permit for a detached garage in Ohio?

Usually. Where a certified residential building department covers the property, a detached garage over 200 square feet needs plan approval and a building permit under the Residential Code of Ohio, and it almost always needs zoning approval from the city, village or township.

What size shed or garage can I build without a permit in Ohio?

The Residential Code of Ohio exempts one-story detached sheds, playhouses and similar buildings of 200 square feet or less (section 102.10). Some cities set a smaller limit, and zoning may still apply.

Do I need a permit for a garage in a township with no building department?

Not a building permit: where no certified residential building department has jurisdiction, the Residential Code of Ohio does not require plan submission, approvals or inspections (section 101.5). Township zoning may still require a zoning permit.
