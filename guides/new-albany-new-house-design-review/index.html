Building a new house in New Albany, Ohio: the design guidelines and the review

A new house in New Albany has to meet the city's Design Guidelines and Requirements as well as zoning and the building code. Outside the Village Center that means one traditional American style followed faithfully, brick or wood on the outside, single-bay garage doors kept back from the front of the house, and wood windows in traditional proportions. The city's residential permit application lists a Certificate of Appropriateness for a new house, and many neighbourhoods add private deed restrictions on top.

By James Kehl, RA, Ohio registered architect, James Kehl Architects. Published 15 November 2026. Rules vary by place: check with your local zoning and building offices.

Which rules apply to a new house in New Albany?

Four sets, and the design has to satisfy all of them:

  • Zoning: the lot's zoning district or the development text for its neighbourhood, which sets setbacks, height and lot coverage.
  • The Design Guidelines and Requirements: section 5 covers houses outside the Village Center, section 2 covers houses in the Village Center, and section 1 describes the traditional American styles the others refer to. All are on the city's website.
  • The Residential Code of Ohio, through the city's building department.
  • Private deed restrictions: many New Albany neighbourhoods carry their own, and some have their own design review. Read the lot's restrictions before design starts.

What style can the house be?

One of the traditional American styles in the guidelines' precedent section, followed faithfully. Section 5 says that "building designs shall not mix elements from different styles", and window and door openings follow the traditional pattern of the style. On individual lots that existed before 1990, a house may instead follow a traditional style found within a quarter mile, with a Certificate of Design Appropriateness.

What do the guidelines say about garages?

They keep the garage from leading the house:

  • garage doors are single-bay, no wider than ten feet;
  • side or rear entry is strongly encouraged;
  • front-facing garage doors sit at least ten feet behind the main front wall;
  • side-load garage doors that face a public street sit at least twenty feet behind the front of the house;
  • garages and outbuildings are no taller than the main roof.

Which materials and windows are allowed?

Wood siding and brick are preferred. Other materials are allowed only where they match traditional wood profiles and textures, and a facade in a material other than brick or wood is reviewed by the Architectural Review Board. Exposed concrete foundation walls are not permitted, and exposed chimneys are brick. Windows are wood, which may be clad in vinyl or aluminum outside. Divided-light windows need vertical panes and properly profiled muntins ("no snap-in or flat muntins will be approved"); one-over-one windows are also accepted, and the same window design is used on every side of the house. Skylights may not be visible from the street.

How tall and how big can it be?

Between one and a half and two and a half stories, with the first-floor entrance at least two feet above grade and the main entrance facing the primary street. The guidelines ask for the scale of the chosen style, which they describe as modest; a larger Colonial Revival or Georgian Revival house is reviewed case by case.

Who reviews the design, and when?

The city's Community Development Department reviews the permit application for zoning and the building code, and its residential application lists a Certificate of Appropriateness for a new house. The Architectural Review Board hears what the guidelines send to it, such as a facade in a material other than brick or wood. The guidelines ask owners to "confer as early as possible with staff" about a planned house, which is good advice: a garage placed wrongly or a style mixed in the first sketches is far cheaper to fix then than after the drawings are done. Where the neighbourhood has its own design review, plan for that approval as well.

How does an architect help with a New Albany house?

By starting the design from the guidelines rather than checking against them at the end, and by drawing what the reviewers look for: the garage setbacks dimensioned, the window and muntin details, the materials named on every elevation. Short answers

Does a new house in New Albany need design review?

Yes. A new house must meet New Albany's Design Guidelines and Requirements; the city's residential application lists a Certificate of Appropriateness for a new house, and the Architectural Review Board hears cases the guidelines send to it, such as facades in materials other than brick or wood.

What style of house is allowed in New Albany, Ohio?

Outside the Village Center, a new house follows one of the traditional American styles in the city's design guidelines without mixing elements of different styles (Design Guidelines and Requirements, section 5).

What are New Albany's garage rules for a new house?

Single-bay garage doors no wider than ten feet; front-facing doors at least ten feet behind the main front wall; side-load doors facing a street at least twenty feet behind the front of the house; side or rear entry strongly encouraged.
