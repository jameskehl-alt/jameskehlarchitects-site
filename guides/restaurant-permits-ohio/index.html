What permits do you need to open a restaurant in Ohio?

Usually four approvals: the local health department's food plan review, a building permit with plans sealed by an Ohio registered architect or engineer, zoning approval for the use, and a liquor permit from the Ohio Division of Liquor Control if the restaurant will serve alcohol. Plans for both the health and building reviews are approved before construction starts.

By James Kehl, RA, Ohio registered architect, James Kehl Architects. Published 15 November 2026. Rules vary by place: check with your local zoning, building and health offices.

Health department plan review

Under the Ohio Uniform Food Safety Code, a new or remodeled food service operation submits plans to its local health department for review and approval before construction begins, and is inspected before the license is issued. Departments ask for a floor plan, an equipment list and layout, the menu, and the finishes of floors, walls and ceilings. Columbus Public Health, for one, asks for plans at least 30 days before construction.

Building permit and sealed drawings

Restaurants fall under the Ohio Building Code, so the building department reviews the plans before a permit is issued, and construction may not begin until they are approved (Ohio Revised Code 3791.04). The same law requires the seal of an Ohio registered architect or engineer on plans that involve technical design analysis, which a restaurant build-out almost always does. Trade permits follow for mechanical, electrical and plumbing work, and the fire alarm and sprinkler contractor submits its own drawings.

How many seats can you have?

Seat count changes the rules. A restaurant with an occupant load under 50 is classed as a business occupancy under the Ohio Building Code; 50 or more makes it an assembly occupancy, with more exits and, depending on size, sprinklers. The occupant load also sets how many restrooms are needed. Work it out before signing a lease.

Kitchen hood and grease interceptor

Cooking that produces grease needs a commercial hood and exhaust under the Ohio Mechanical Code and, in most places, a grease interceptor under the Ohio Plumbing Code. In an existing building these can be the most expensive items to add, because of the duct route to the roof and the work in the floor slab.

Zoning and change of use

If the space was not a restaurant before, the zoning code must allow one there, and the change of use is reviewed by zoning and the building department. In Columbus, for example, zoning clearance is required before a change in use. Parking, signs and patios often have their own rules.

Liquor permit

To serve alcohol, the owner applies to the Ohio Division of Liquor Control for the right permit class. Some permit classes are limited by quota, and the location's wet or dry status matters, so start early.

Short answers

Do I need health department approval before building a restaurant in Ohio?

Yes. Under the Ohio Uniform Food Safety Code, plans for a new or remodeled food service operation are reviewed and approved by the local health department before construction begins.

Do restaurant plans need an architect's seal in Ohio?

Generally, yes. Ohio Revised Code 3791.04 requires the seal of an Ohio registered architect or engineer on plans for buildings under the Ohio Building Code when the work involves technical design analysis.

Does a small restaurant count as assembly occupancy?

Not under 50 people. A restaurant with an occupant load of fewer than 50 is classed as a business occupancy under the Ohio Building Code; 50 or more makes it assembly.
