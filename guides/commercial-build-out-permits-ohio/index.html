What permits does a commercial build-out need in Ohio?

Plan approval and a building permit from the building department before work starts, usually with drawings sealed by an Ohio registered architect or engineer, then trade permits, inspections and a certificate of occupancy. If the use of the space changes, zoning approval comes first.

By James Kehl, RA, Ohio registered architect, James Kehl Architects. Published 15 November 2026. Rules vary by place: check with your local zoning, building and health offices.

Which code applies?

Commercial buildings in Ohio fall under the Ohio Building Code. Since March 1, 2024, the 2024 Ohio Building Code and the 2024 Ohio Existing Building Code have been in force, based on the 2021 International Building Code and International Existing Building Code. The existing building code governs alterations and changes of occupancy in buildings already standing, which is what most build-outs are.

Do the drawings need a seal?

Usually. Ohio Revised Code 3791.04 requires the seal of an Ohio registered architect or engineer on plans for these buildings, with exceptions for replacement equipment of similar type and capacity and for work that does not involve technical design analysis. New walls that change exits, new restrooms, and changes to building systems generally do involve it. Corporate or firm seals are not accepted in Ohio: the seal is an individual architect's or engineer's.

When can work start?

Only after the plans are approved. The same law says construction or alteration may not begin until plan approval is issued, and an approval lapses if work does not start within twelve months.

What does a change of use add?

A space moving from one kind of use to another, such as an office becoming a fitness studio or a shop becoming a café, is a change of occupancy. Zoning must allow the new use, and the building may need upgrades to exits, fire separations, restrooms, accessibility or sprinklers to meet the code for the new use. Some cities require a change of use permit even when no construction is planned.

Accessibility in an alteration

New work must meet current accessibility requirements. When an alteration affects the main area of a business, the existing building code can also require improvements to the accessible route, restrooms and drinking fountains serving it, within a cost limit tied to the alteration. Plan for it in the budget.

Before you sign the lease

Have an architect look at the space and the planned use. A short review shows what the building department will expect, and the lease can then say who pays for it: the landlord's work, the tenant's work, and any allowance.

Short answers

Do I need a permit to build out a commercial space in Ohio?

Yes, for anything beyond cosmetic work. Plans are reviewed under the Ohio Building Code and must be approved before construction or alteration begins.

Can a contractor draw the plans for a commercial build-out in Ohio?

Only for work that does not involve technical design analysis. Otherwise Ohio Revised Code 3791.04 requires the seal of an Ohio registered architect or engineer.

Which building code applies to existing commercial buildings in Ohio?

The 2024 Ohio Existing Building Code, in force since March 1, 2024 alongside the 2024 Ohio Building Code, governs alterations and changes of occupancy in existing buildings.
