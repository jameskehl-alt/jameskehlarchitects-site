About James Kehl Architects

James Kehl Architects is an architecture practice in Ohio, led by James Kehl, RA, an Ohio registered architect. The firm draws new homes, additions and renovations, pole barns and zoning site plans, and commercial spaces such as tenant improvements, restaurants and stores, for owners anywhere in the state, working remotely first with a site visit when the job needs one.

Who is the architect?

James Kehl, RA, the principal and owner, is an Ohio registered architect. Before founding the practice in 2023 he spent more than a decade at studios in New York, Los Angeles and Minneapolis, on high-end houses and on residential, commercial, educational and cultural buildings across the country. At HGA he was an Associate in the arts, community and education practice, on work that received an AIA Minnesota Honor Award.

What does the practice do and not do?

It designs and draws new custom homes, additions and renovations, pole barns and post-frame buildings, zoning site plans, and commercial spaces: tenant improvements for offices, salons and clinics, restaurants, cafés and bars, and retail stores, including store sets for brands opening several locations. All of it is in Ohio. It prepares its own drawings, and every seal it applies is on its own work. It does not work outside Ohio.

How does a job run?

A first call or the inquiry form; a short written agreement with the scope; drawings developed with you, remotely first and on site when needed; and help through the zoning and building reviews.

Does the firm do pro bono work?

Yes, on a small scale. Each year James Kehl Architects takes on one pro bono project for an Ohio nonprofit, church or community group: usually a feasibility study or concept design that shows what a building or space could become, roughly what it would cost, and what to put in front of a board or donors. If the project goes ahead, the firm can take it through permit drawings under a normal agreement. Groups can ask through the inquiry form; say it is a pro bono request.
